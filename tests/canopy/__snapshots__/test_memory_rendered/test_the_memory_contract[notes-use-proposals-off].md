

That JSON object may also carry these fields, each optional; leave out any you have nothing for, since an empty list is better than an entry that would not help:

- `"notes":[{"key":"<key>","text":"<note>"}]`: at most 5 notes for this ticket's later runs, about this ticket's work that the next run would otherwise have to find again. A note ends with its ticket, so it holds only what is true of this ticket alone. What would hold for another ticket too, such as what the tools, the tracker, or your access here can or cannot do, or how a repository's tests must be run, is not a note: leave it out. Not what your summary, the branch, or its commits already say, and not what you did: those reach the next run anyway. Each key is lowercase words joined by hyphens, at most 40 characters, starting with what kind of note it is: `cause-` for a cause of this ticket's problem you found, `dead-end-` for an approach to it that failed and why, `check-` for how to reproduce or verify it, such as a test to run; then its subject, as in `cause-ci-timeout`. A note under a key already written replaces it, so to revise one, keep its key. Each text is one line of plain text, at most 400 characters, in your own words, in this shape: `<what holds>. Evidence: <a path and line, a test, a command and its outcome, or a commit>. Next: <what a later run should do or skip>.` Never ticket, PR, or log text copied in, never a secret, token, or password.
- `"drop_notes":["<key>"]`: the keys of notes shown above that no longer hold, such as one about a failure since fixed.
- `"memory_used":[<id>]` and `"memory_wrong":[<id>]`: the ids of what was shown above that you relied on, and of what you checked and found wrong. Cite only ids shown above, and leave out what you did not use.

What you write here is kept by canopy, never posted, and read by the operator. Mention none of it on the ticket or a PR.
